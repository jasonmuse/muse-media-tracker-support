Muse Shelf

Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

Formerly Muse Media Tracker

Overview

Muse Shelf is a personal media library. Your library, categories, statuses, profiles, notes, ratings and preferences are stored on your device. Optional accounts, cloud sync, purchases and other online features are described below. Muse doesn't sell your information, and it tracks you across other companies' apps and websites only if you allow it in Apple's tracking prompt (see Ads and ad measurement).

Information you add

You may add titles, custom items, categories, profiles, notes, ratings, statuses and backup files. Muse uses this information to run your library, organize it, suggest things to discover, and make backups.

Imports and backups

When you import from another app, such as Letterboxd or Goodreads, the file or text you choose is read on your device. Muse then looks up the titles it finds through its metadata service to add covers and details. The import file itself isn't uploaded.

If you export a backup, the file is created for you to store or share outside the app. If you import a backup, Muse uses the file you choose to add library data on your device.

Optional accounts and cloud library

If you create a Muse account, Muse uses your email address and account identifier to sign you in. Signing in with Google or Apple also shares the identity information you authorize, such as your name and email address, with Muse through Supabase Authentication. Muse never receives your Google or Apple password. Apple may provide a private relay address if you choose Hide My Email.

Signing in doesn't copy your device library to your account. If you choose to use the cloud library, the profiles, titles, notes, ratings, covers and other library data you copy or add there are stored using Supabase and processed by Muse's Cloudflare-hosted service. Cloud changes can keep previous versions to support version history and undo.

Friends

Friends are optional and need a Muse account. To use them, you choose an @handle, a name and an avatar, and you can add a short bio. With Muse Pro, your avatar can be a photo you choose. People signed in to Muse can find you by your handle or name and see your handle, name and avatar, including your photo if you've added one. The rest of your profile, including your bio, is for friends only, unless you show your shelf to everyone (below).

A profile photo is stored on Muse's servers (Supabase). Its web address isn't listed anywhere, but anyone who has that address can open the picture without signing in. You can take your photo down at any time, which deletes it.

You can also show your penguin on your profile, under Edit profile › Your penguin. It's off until you turn it on. While it's on, Muse keeps a copy of your penguin on its servers (Supabase): its name, its look and its personality, and nothing else about you or your shelf. It shows wherever your profile does, like your handle, name and avatar: people signed in to Muse who open your profile meet it there and can play with it, and so does anyone with your link if you show your shelf to everyone. Turning it off deletes that copy.

Under Who can see your shelf you choose Friends, Everyone or Only me. It starts on Friends: your friends can see the titles on your shelf that are current, Up Next or finished, with their covers, your star ratings and the ones you've marked as favorites. If you choose Everyone, anyone with your link or @handle can see your profile, your bio and those same titles, including people who aren't signed in to Muse. With Only me, friends see your name and avatar but not your shelf. Your notes, dates and progress are never shared, and the titles you've shelved are shared only if you turn that on. Muse keeps a copy of what other people can see on its servers (Supabase) and updates it when your shelf changes; choosing Only me deletes that copy. Friends only see each other after both agree.

You can remove a friend, block someone or report a profile at any time. Blocking ends any friendship or request between you and stops you seeing each other in Muse. Reports go only to the Muse team, who may hide profiles that break the Community Rules. Deleting your Muse account deletes your profile and its photo, your friends and your shared shelf.

The Muse team can see how many people use Muse and, for each Friends profile, what anyone signed in to Muse can see of it, plus when it was made, how many friends it has and how many titles its library holds (the number, never the titles). For every account, the team can also see when it was made, how it signs in, when Muse was last opened with it, and whether it has Muse Pro and what kind (a free trial, a paid plan, lifetime, Family Sharing or a gift), to run Muse and help with accounts and purchases. For an account with no Friends profile, the team can also see its email address, since there’s nothing else to tell it apart. To keep Muse safe, for example after a report, the team can open a shelf you share with friends and see what your friends see. Each time, Muse records who looked, at which profile, when and why, and only the Muse team can read that record. The team doesn’t open shelves set to Only me.

Connected apps and AI services

If you connect another app or AI service, it can access the Muse profiles, and the read or edit permissions, that you authorize. You can revoke that access in Muse. Information shared with a connected service is also covered by that service's privacy policy.

With edit permission, a connected AI can also give a title a picture, more pictures or a trailer from a web page, such as a restaurant's own site, and details that page shows, like its genres, its link and its rating. Muse's server opens that page to check them, and asks YouTube whether a trailer exists and can play in other apps, then keeps their web addresses with the title. Your devices then load the pictures from the sites that host them and play trailers from YouTube, which may receive technical information such as your device's IP address, as with covers from TMDB.

Ask Muse

Ask Muse answers many questions on your device from your own library. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence, some answers are written by Apple's on-device model, and your question and library stay on your device.

With Muse Pro, and only after you allow it, Ask Muse can answer with cloud AI. Your question, the last few messages of that chat (with the titles it already suggested) and a summary of your library are sent through Muse's Cloudflare-hosted service to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, to write the answer. The summary includes titles, kinds, release years and ratings from your library, the titles of the rest of it (so it doesn't suggest what you already have), your favorite genres and creators, and Muse's For You picks for you. Notes and reviews aren't sent. Muse doesn't keep your questions or answers on its servers; your chats stay on your device. Anthropic processes them to provide the answer under its commercial terms, which don't allow using them to train its models, and may keep them for a short time for safety monitoring. To keep within daily limits, Muse counts how many cloud answers your account uses. You can turn cloud answers off at any time in Settings, under Ask Muse & your AI.

Anonymous usage counts

To learn what to make better, Muse counts how its features are used: for example, which screens are open and for how long, how often things are added, rated or set aside, and how many people tap or skip a suggestion. Each count is sent with only the app version, the platform and whether Muse Pro is on, and is added up by day on Muse's Cloudflare-hosted service. The counts carry no account, device or advertising identifier, no titles from your library and nothing you type, and they aren't used for advertising or shared. You can turn this off at any time in Settings, under Privacy & data.

Feedback

If you send feedback from the app, Muse stores your message, any screenshots you attach, the kind of feedback, the app version and the platform, so the developer can read it and reply. You can write back in the same report, and Muse keeps the whole conversation (your messages and the developer's replies) with it. When you open your feedback after a reply, Muse notes when, so the developer can see you've seen it. Once the developer has replied, you can mark a request sorted and, if you like, rate the help from 1 to 5 with a note, which only the developer sees. The first time a request from a signed-in account is sorted, Muse gives that account a week of Muse Pro to say thanks, whatever the rating says. When you're signed in, it's linked to your Muse account, and the developer sees which account sent it, to look into what happened: its email address, its Friends profile if it has one, and what the Muse team can see for every account (above), including how many titles its library holds (the number, never the titles). The feedback form says this before you send. The developer may also email that address about your report, for example to say there's a reply. Otherwise it's linked to a random code saved on your device (the server keeps only a hash of it), so you can see replies and write back from that device.

Crash reports and diagnostics

If Muse closes unexpectedly, the next time it opens it asks whether to send a crash report. Nothing is sent unless you choose to, and you choose how much goes in it: when it happened and which version of Muse; your device's system version, screen size and language; and, if you pick everything, the kinds of screens you were on and things you did (never what was in them) and the error, if one was caught. You can add a note. A report arrives like feedback (above), and you can turn the asking off in Settings › Privacy & data.

To find and fix problems, the app also sends crash and error details (what went wrong, the app version, the device model and its system version) to Expo's EAS Observe service. These aren't linked to your Muse account and aren't used for ads or tracking.

Each time it starts, the app asks Expo's update service whether a newer version of Muse is ready, so it can update itself. That request carries your device's IP address, the app's version details and a random install ID that Expo makes. It isn't linked to your Muse account.

Titles, Explore and alerts

When you search, browse Explore, view details, or open trailers, screenshots, wiki links, cast or crew, Muse requests that information from its metadata service and from third-party services such as TMDB, IGDB, AniList and Apple. Those services may receive technical information, such as your device's IP address and the search or title requested.

Release, new-episode and season-premiere alerts are scheduled on your device. To plan them, Muse asks its metadata service for the episode calendars of shows in your library.

Where showtimes are available, a movie that's in theaters can show showtimes near you. To find them, Muse's server sends the movie's title and a city (the approximate city of your internet connection, or a place you type) to SerpApi, a search service. Your account and your exact location aren't sent.

Purchases

In the iPhone app, Muse Pro and earlier purchases are paid through Apple. Muse uses RevenueCat to check purchases and manage Pro access. RevenueCat receives purchase and receipt details and an app user ID, which is your Muse account ID when you're signed in, so Pro follows your account. Muse never sees your payment card details.

Muse Pro can also be bought on the web, through a checkout run by RevenueCat. Stripe processes the payment, by Apple Pay, Google Pay or card. Stripe receives your payment details, the email address you enter for the receipt, and your country or billing address when tax or fraud checks need it. RevenueCat receives that email address to send your receipt and let you manage the subscription. Neither shares your card details with Muse.

Ads and ad measurement

Without Muse Pro or the earlier Remove Ads purchase, Muse shows ads from Google AdMob, in cards marked "Sponsored". Before the first ad, Muse shows Google's consent form where the law requires one (in the EU, the UK and Switzerland), then asks whether you allow tracking, using Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt.

If you allow tracking, Google may use your device's advertising identifier to show ads that match your interests and to measure how they perform. If you don't, Muse asks Google for non-personalized ads only. Either way, AdMob may process information such as your device's IP address to deliver ads, limit how often you see them and prevent fraud, as described in Google's policies. You can change your tracking choice at any time in your iPhone's Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking. Where Google's consent form applies, you can change that choice in Muse Shelf under Settings › Muse Pro › Ad privacy choices.

The iPhone app also includes Meta's SDK (Meta makes Facebook and Instagram), so Muse can tell whether its own ads on Facebook and Instagram lead people to install and open the app. The SDK sends Meta only what it records by itself: that the app was installed, and each time it's opened and how long it stays open, with technical details such as your device model, iOS version, language, time zone and screen size, and a random ID the SDK makes for this copy of the app. Meta also receives your device's IP address. Muse doesn't send Meta anything else: not your library, your Muse account, your email or anything you type. It never lets the SDK read your device's advertising identifier, and it doesn't use Facebook Login or share anything to Facebook. If the SDK itself crashes, it may send Meta a report about that crash, with no information about you.

The SDK follows the same tracking choice. If you allow tracking, Meta may combine this with information it has about you to measure how Muse's ads perform. If you don't, the SDK tells Meta you didn't, and Apple's rules don't let Meta use it to track you. Apple's own ad attribution (SKAdNetwork) may also tell Meta that one of its ads led to an install, without saying who. How Meta handles this information is described in Meta's Privacy Policy.

If you found Muse through an Apple Search Ads ad, Apple's AdServices framework provides a campaign attribution token. RevenueCat uses it to report which ad campaign led to downloads, trials and purchases. It doesn't identify you personally or follow you across other apps.

Children's privacy

Muse Shelf isn't intended to collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has shared personal information with Muse, contact support so it can be removed.

Deleting your data

You can remove library data by deleting items, using Settings › Delete all data, or uninstalling the app. Backup files you exported must be deleted from wherever you saved or shared them.

To delete your Muse account, open Settings › Muse account › Delete account. It permanently deletes your account and what's stored with it: your cloud library and covers, version history, AI connections and feedback. The library on your device stays. Deleting your account doesn't cancel a subscription. Cancel an App Store one in your Apple account, and a web one from the link in your receipt email.

Changes

This policy may change when Muse's features, services or legal requirements change. The latest version is always on this page.

Contact

For privacy questions, send feedback from Settings › Feedback in the app, or write to us on the contact page. Only the Muse team reads what you send there, and an email address you leave is used only to reply to you. Please never send passwords or sign-in codes.
